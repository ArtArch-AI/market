# Beat and Look Reference

Use this reference to select a story shape and maintain one coherent collage
language. Keep exact model capabilities in the live ArtArch node catalog, not here.

## Narrative arcs

| Arc | Use for | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hook-payoff` | one surprising idea | hook, context, build, payoff, button |
| `how-it-works` | mechanism or system | hook, definition, steps, consequence, takeaway |
| `myth-buster` | correcting a belief | fact, myth, failure, replacement model, takeaway |
| `timeline` | history or evolution | origin, events, turn, present, implication |
| `problem-solution` | risk or practical guidance | problem, stakes, mechanism, proof, action |

Put the audience promise in the first three seconds. For roughly 30 seconds use
six to eight compact beats; for roughly 60 seconds use about six narrated beats
with two shots each, or ten to twelve single-shot beats. Change the visual every
three to six seconds and avoid any shot longer than about seven seconds unless
the delivery format demands it.

## Shot grammar

Use a wide or medium poster to establish the relationship, then a close or detail
poster to show the decisive object, number, or action. Choose one move per shot:

- `static`: reserve for the payoff, warning, quote, or key number;
- `push-in`: focus attention or increase pressure;
- `pull-out`: reveal a system or consequence;
- `pan` or `tilt`: traverse a process, list, or timeline;
- `parallax`: make separated paper layers feel alive without bending them.

Do not repeat the same move in adjacent shots. Element motion is independent of
camera motion: several rigid pieces may slide, pivot, scatter, flutter, pulse, or
settle together. Avoid organic melting, body distortion, and uncontrolled text
motion.

## Collage image prompt

Compose prompts from five blocks:

1. **Style:** mixed-media editorial paper collage, torn or scissor-cut edges,
   tape, halftone or newsprint texture, flat printed shapes, physical shadows.
2. **Pieces:** name the subject, prop, evidence, labels, and decorative scraps as
   separate layered cut-outs.
3. **Background:** choose one bold flat paper color with sufficient contrast.
4. **Headline:** use a short exact phrase in quotes and state its typographic
   treatment. Keep critical copy available for post-production overlay.
5. **Output:** state the verified aspect ratio and resolution; request a straight-on
   scanned or flat-lay composition rather than CGI depth.

Reuse style, paper texture, edge language, typography family, and shadow behavior
across every beat. Let background colors travel with the emotional arc.

Useful topic-matched directions include clean Swiss editorial for technical and
financial mechanisms, newsprint editorial for current affairs, punk photocopy for
rebellion, constructivist diagonals for conflict and industry, and period-specific
print languages for history. Pick by topic and tone, not narration language.

## H3 first-and-last-frame pair

Treat a pair as two states of one physical paper set:

- lock poster dimensions, composition, palette, headline, and object identity;
- move only the pieces required by the narrated action;
- keep changes small enough to interpolate without inventing depth;
- give the last frame a settled endpoint rather than a loop reset;
- start a new pair for a new semantic beat or scene.

Example for a short-selling explainer: the first frame shows a borrowed stock
certificate beside a 100-yuan price tag; the last frame keeps the same poster and
headline while the certificate slides toward a market counter and the cash cut-out
settles in the seller's hand. The later buyback is a different beat and therefore
a different pair.

## Motion prompt

Describe motion rather than redescribing the image. Specify:

- shot size and a single slow camera move;
- paper-native movement with modest amplitude and clear ownership;
- one physical light change, such as a soft surface sweep or shadow drift;
- flat 2D rigidity, stable typography, and preserved layout;
- a single continuous shot ending in a named settled state.

Keep motion away from critical lettering. If exact text matters, overlay it after
generation and verify it in extracted frames.
